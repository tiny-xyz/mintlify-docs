The venue has no strategy concept — the **account** is the unit of
everything: funds, risk, resting orders, and the heartbeat deadman's
sweep. That gives a clean isolation rule and one operational pattern.

## Rule one: one account per strategy

A sweep, a `cancel_all`, and a balance are all account-wide. If two
strategies share an account, they share fate: one strategy's deadman
lapse clears the other's orders, and their PnL is indistinguishable.
Give each strategy its own account; the operator plane below makes the
fan-out cheap.

## The shape: REST + heartbeats + one event socket

A runner driving N strategies runs:

- **REST for trading** — one operator-scope token acts for any
  ledger-known account per request (the body's `account` is the
  effective account), so N strategies trade concurrently over plain
  HTTP with nothing per-account to keep alive. Without operator scope,
  each account's own client token works the same way for that one
  account.
- **One heartbeat loop** — `POST /v1/heartbeat` arms and re-anchors up
  to 10 accounts per frame under one window; a runner beats its whole
  set in ⌈N/10⌉ requests per cadence tick. This loop — not any socket
  — is the runner's liveness signal.
- **One operator events session** — a single account-edge WebSocket
  that `listen`s every trading account: order lifecycle, fills, fees,
  and balance events for every listened account arrive here, each
  frame tagged with its account and stamped with that account's
  `event_seq`.

Trading authority, order protection, and event delivery are three
independent planes: listening is delivery only, and losing the event
socket neither disarms nor fires anything.

### Listening is batched

`listen` takes up to **10 accounts per frame** — distinct, canonical:

```json
{"version":9,"type":"listen","body":{"accounts":["...01","...02","...03"]}}
```

Verdicts are **per account**, in frame order: one refused account
voids nothing around it, and each admitted account answers an empty
confirmation bracket (`snapshot_begin`, then `snapshot_end` naming the
account with zero counts). The delivery tier restates no state —
reconcile each account from `GET /v1/orders`, then splice its stream
above the snapshot's `event_seq` anchor. Pipeline more frames for more
accounts; the full contract is in
[the account events WebSocket](/account-events#operator-multiplexing).

## One heartbeat loop for all of them

Dead-runner protection is the per-account deadman, armed and fed over
REST — see
[the heartbeat deadman](/rest-trading#the-heartbeat-deadman) for exact
semantics. The batch is judged whole, one window per frame; a runner
wanting different windows for different accounts pipelines frames.

Orders a strategy places while its account is armed are covered by the
standing deadline — REST trading activity never counts as liveness.
Only beats do.

## If the runner dies, every strategy's orders clear

1. The runner beats accounts A, B, C on a 30 s window and trades over
   REST.
2. The runner process dies. Beats stop; nothing else changes — no
   socket teardown is involved in protection.
3. Each account's deadline lapses where it stood. The window is a
   floor — never early.
4. **Recovery path:** the runner restarts inside the window and beats
   A, B, C again. The beat re-anchors each deadline; resting orders
   survived. Reconcile each account from its snapshot, splice the
   stream, resume.
5. **Death path:** no beat arrives. At the venue's first tick at or
   past each deadline, that account is swept: every resting order
   comes back as `cancel_done` with `cause: "session_lost"` on the
   account-events stream, and collateral unlocks on the confirmed
   remaining. The fire consumes the arm — a later restart finds the
   accounts unprotected and re-arms with its first beat.

A restarted **venue** keeps every arm (arming is durable) but grants
one fresh window from boot before any sweep can fire — a runner that
keeps beating through a venue outage never notices; one that died with
the venue is swept one window after boot.

## Alternative: no deadman

Protection is opt-in per account. A strategy that prefers its resting
orders to survive its own crashes simply never arms — orders then rest
until filled, cancelled, expired, or the market closes. There is no
per-connection protection to fall back to; the deadman is the whole
story.
