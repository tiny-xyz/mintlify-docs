---
title: "Running more than one strategy"
description: "One operator session anchors liveness and dead-runner protection for every strategy."
---

The venue has no strategy concept — the **account** is the unit of
everything: funds, risk, resting orders, and the cancel-on-disconnect
sweep. That gives a clean isolation rule and one operational pattern.

## Rule one: one account per strategy

A sweep, a `cancel_all`, and a balance are all account-wide. If two
strategies share an account, they share fate: one strategy's
disconnect protection clears the other's orders, and their PnL is
indistinguishable. Give each strategy its own account; the operator
plane below makes the fan-out cheap.

## The shape: one operator session, N trading accounts

A runner driving N strategies runs:

- **One operator WS session** — authenticated with an operator-scope
  token — that `listen`s every trading account. This single
  connection is the runner's liveness anchor and its event feed:
  order lifecycle, fills, fees, and balance events for every listened
  account arrive here, each frame tagged with its account.
- **REST for trading** — orders and cancels are per-request
  authenticated (`Authorization: Tiny-v1 HEX` with each account's own
  token), so N strategies trade concurrently over plain HTTP with no
  per-account socket to keep alive.

One connection to keep alive, one place to read events, N accounts
trading.

### Listening is batched

`listen` takes up to **10 accounts per frame** — distinct, nonzero:

```json
{"version":8,"type":"listen","body":{"accounts":["...01","...02","...03"]}}
```

Verdicts are **per account**, in frame order: one refused account
voids nothing around it, and each admitted account's baseline
bracket — closed by its `snapshot_end` — **is** the subscribed
confirmation. Reconcile from the bracket, then trust the stream.
The cap is the frame budget, not venue policy: pipeline more frames
for more accounts. Listens bill the *operator's* rate allowance, so
a reconnecting runner re-listens hundreds of accounts within a
couple of rate windows. The full frame contract is in
[the private trading WebSocket](/websocket-trading#automatic-snapshot).

## One heartbeat for all of them

The wire heartbeat is per connection — WS ping/pong on each socket,
documented in [the trading WebSocket](/websocket-trading) — so the operator session is the only
socket the runner must keep responsive. There is no per-account
keepalive.

Dead-runner protection rides the same session: the
`cancel_on_disconnect_ms` on the **operator's auth** covers **every
account it goes on to listen** — nothing rides the listen frames.

## If the runner dies, every strategy's orders clear

When the operator session ends and no reclaim happens inside the
window, the venue sweeps **each listened account**: a venue-authored
`cancel_all` per account, per-order `cancel_done` events with
`cause: "session_lost"`, funds unlocked on confirmed remaining —
journaled before publishing, so even a venue crash mid-sweep
completes on recovery.

The sweep conditions, per account — each is a deliberate handoff
rule, not a loophole:

| An account is swept only if... | Why |
|---|---|
| the dying session was its **last watcher** — no other operator listening, no live client WS session for it | protection hands off to a surviving watcher instead of firing under it |
| it was still **listened** at the end — not explicitly `unlisten`ed earlier | unlisten is a live runner's choice to release an account, not a lapse |
| it has at least one **open restable order** | an empty book arms nothing |

Reconnecting inside the window and re-listening an account **disarms
its pending sweep** — the runner reclaimed it. An account already
armed when the operator unbinds keeps its standing deadline.

Orders a strategy places over REST while its account's sweep is armed
are **included** in the sweep when it fires — REST activity never
counts as liveness.

## The failure drill, end to end

1. Runner holds one operator session (window, say, 10 000 ms) and has
   listened accounts A, B, C. Strategies trade over REST.
2. The runner process dies. The operator socket drops.
3. The venue arms one deadline per listened account. The window is a
   floor — never early.
4. **Recovery path:** the runner restarts within 10 s, re-auths, and
   re-listens A, B, C. Each admitted listen disarms that account's
   sweep. Resting orders survived; the runner reconciles from its
   snapshot bracket.
5. **Death path:** no reclaim. At the first venue heartbeat at or
   past each deadline, A, B, and C are each swept — every resting
   order across all three accounts comes back as `cancel_done` with
   `cause: "session_lost"`, and collateral unlocks.

A restarted **venue** (not runner) drops armed deadlines with the
sessions themselves; protection resumes when the runner re-auths and
re-listens — so a runner's reconnect loop should always re-listen its
full set, unconditionally.

## Alternative: one client session per account

Without operator scope, the same protection exists per account: each
account's own WS session with a nonzero window arms when that
account's **last** session closes. It works, but it is N sockets, N
liveness domains, and N reconnect loops — the redundancy the operator
plane exists to remove. Prefer it only for a single-strategy runner.

## Verified live

Every behavioral claim above is pinned by an end-to-end probe against
the deployed one-box exchange: one operator session listening six
accounts; its death swept every last-watcher account's book inside
the window, while a live client session, a second operator's listen,
an explicit unlisten, and a reconnect-plus-re-listen each kept their
account's orders exactly as described.
