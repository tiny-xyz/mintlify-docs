All trading is REST. Each command is one HTTP `POST` whose body is a single
canonical frame, authenticated by the `Tiny-v1` header on that request. There
is no trading WebSocket and no trading session: identity, scope, and policy
ride each request.

## Command doors

Each route accepts exactly one frame type; any other type answers
`bad_request` with reason `bad_type`.

| Route | Frame type | Acknowledgement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `POST /v1/orders` | `new_order` | `202` with an `order_submitted` body carrying the exchange `order_id` |
| `POST /v1/cancel` | `cancel_order` | Bodyless `202` after durable publication |
| `POST /v1/cancel-all` | `cancel_all` | Bodyless `202` after every expanded cancel durably published |
| `POST /v1/modify` | `modify_order` | Bodyless `202` after durable publication |
| `POST /v1/heartbeat` | `heartbeat` | Bodyless `202`: the whole batch armed, re-anchored, or disarmed |

Every `202` means durably accepted, never executed: engine verdicts
(`order_accepted`, fills, `cancel_done`, `modify_done`, asynchronous
`order_rejected`) arrive on
[the account events WebSocket](/account-events) or in the next snapshot.

Refusals map to status codes: `422` carries an `order_rejected` frame, `400` a
`bad_request` frame, `401` an `auth_failed` frame, and `429` a rate refusal
with a `Retry-After` header. `503` means ingress is saturated (not admitted —
retry). `504` means the response rendezvous timed out and the command may have
executed: retry new orders as the same bytes, cancels and heartbeats freely,
modifies with the same `modify_seq`.

## Request mechanics

- Send a `Content-Length`; chunked transfer is refused. Bodies over 587 bytes
  answer `413` before parsing.
- The token is re-verified per request, including once more after the body
  arrives — a token can expire between requests on one keep-alive connection.
- Rate limits charge the token's account (30 requests per second per account
  by default) plus a per-connection limiter.
- `GET` on a command route answers `405`.

## Snapshots

`GET /v1/orders` returns the token account's complete state as NDJSON, one
canonical frame per line: `snapshot_begin`, one `market_state` per live
market, one `snapshot_order` per open order, one `snapshot_position` per
market holding lots, `snapshot_balance`, and `snapshot_end` with integrity
counts — verify them.

`snapshot_end` also carries the account's `event_seq` anchor: every
account-events frame at or below it is already reflected in the snapshot, so
resume a buffered stream strictly above it. This anchor is the one recovery
coordinate in the system — snapshot first, then splice.

`GET /v1/orders/{account}` (the exact canonical 40-hex address) is the
operator cross-account form. Client tokens see `404`, never `403`: the
refusal must not confirm an account exists.

`GET /v1/status` answers a `venue_status` frame with no token and no rate
charge. An answering front has a live command path; absence of an answer is
the degraded signal.

## The heartbeat deadman

`POST /v1/heartbeat` is the only order-protection mechanism: transport
disconnects arm nothing and fire nothing. A beat names 1–10 accounts and one
`window_ms`:

```json
{"version":9,"type":"heartbeat","body":{"salt":"7","accounts":["000000000000000000000000000000000000002a"],"window_ms":30000}}
```

- **Arm / re-anchor** — a nonzero window (`3000..=600000` ms) sets each named
  account's deadline to now + window. While armed, the venue sweeps the
  account the moment a window elapses with no further beat naming it: every
  resting order terminates with `cancel_done` cause `session_lost`, and funds
  unlock on the confirmed remaining.
- **Disarm** — `window_ms` `0`. Disarming an unarmed account is an idempotent
  `202`.
- **All or nothing** — the batch is judged whole: every named account arms or
  none does. A client token must name exactly its own account (anything else
  rejects `wrong_account`); an operator token names any ledger-known accounts
  (an unknown one rejects `unknown_account`). The reject echoes your `salt`,
  the first refused account, and the all-zero `market_id`.
- **One window per frame** — different windows for different accounts means
  pipelining frames.

### Sweep semantics

- The window is a floor: the venue fires no earlier than the deadline, at its
  next internal tick at or past it.
- A sweep is a venue-authored cancel-all: each swept order answers its
  ordinary terminal `cancel_done` with cause `session_lost`. Your own cancel
  crossing the sweep in flight still answers `requested` — you did ask.
- A fire consumes the arm. After a sweep, the account is unprotected until
  your next beat re-arms it — a recovering runner's first action is a fresh
  beat.
- An account with nothing resting still arms and still fires; the lapse just
  sweeps nothing. Orders placed while armed are covered by the standing
  deadline — REST trading activity never counts as liveness. Only beats do.

### Restart behavior

Arming is durable: a window change survives a gateway restart. Deadlines are
not — on boot the venue re-anchors every armed account at boot time plus its
window, so an outage grants one full window to resume beating and never
sweeps at time zero.

### Choosing a window and cadence

Beat at a third to half the window, and size the window to absorb your retry
budget plus network jitter: a 30 s window with a 10 s beat cadence survives
two lost beats. Sub-jitter windows are refused at decode
(`bad_heartbeat_window` below 3 s), and windows above 10 minutes answer
nothing a deadman can mean.

<Warning>
Do not arm a short window and then submit orders from a process that cannot
keep beating. The sweep cancels every resting order of the account — that is
its purpose.
</Warning>
